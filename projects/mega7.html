Decomposing the Magnificent Seven

Return, risk, co-movement and index concentration of Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Nvidia, Meta and Tesla since May 2012, with an event study of Nvidia’s November 2026 sell-off.

Explore the Dashboard View the Code on GitHub

The study takes apart the seven large US stocks often called the Magnificent Seven over their common sample, 21 May 2012 to 10 November 2026: how each one’s return compares with its risk, how closely the seven move together over time and in the tails, how much of the S&P 500’s variance they explain, and how Nvidia’s November 2026 sell-off splits into market, sector and company-specific parts.

Prices come from Yahoo Finance and are not redistributed, so this page shows summary statistics only: the metrics, correlation matrices, month-end rolling statistics and event-day figures that the repository publishes in results/. The sample is chosen with hindsight. These seven were picked because they became the largest US companies, so their high returns, Sharpe ratios and alphas describe what happened to seven survivors, and say nothing about a strategy anyone could have followed in 2012.

Risk and Return

Only Nvidia stands out on a risk-adjusted basis: its Sharpe ratio is 1.17, against 0.81 for SPY, and five of the seven are within 0.1 of SPY. The equal-weight basket’s Sharpe ratio, 1.21, is higher than any single stock’s, because the seven’s average pairwise correlation is only 0.46.

Correlation Over Time

How much the seven trade as one varies a lot over time and is low now. The one-year average pairwise correlation ranged from 0.16 (December 2013) to 0.68 (July 2020) at month ends, and is 0.26 on 10 November 2026. Its fall from 0.50 at the end of March 2026 to 0.31 at the end of April is largely a window effect: 9 April 2025, when SPY rose 10.5%, left the window on 13 April 2026.

Series

Correlations over the trailing 252 trading days, sampled at each month end from May 2013, the first full window, to 10 November 2026. Correlations between pairs of the seven are published over time only as their average, so the single-pair view shows each stock against SPY.

Correlation Matrix

Correlations are lower at longer horizons: the average of the 21 pairs is 0.46 daily, 0.42 weekly and 0.40 monthly. After the Fama–French five factors and momentum are removed, the average residual correlation is 0.04. Only GOOGL–AMZN (0.20), GOOGL–META (0.16) and AMZN–META (0.16) remain above 0.1.

Returns

Calm and Stressed Days

On SPY’s worst 10% of days the average pairwise correlation is 0.41, against 0.25 on the middle 80%. A one-factor model in which only SPY’s variance changes predicts 0.48 for those worst days, so the rise in sell-offs is no more than the market’s higher volatility explains.

The benchmark keeps each stock’s full-sample beta to SPY and the full-sample residual covariance, and replaces SPY’s variance with its variance on those days. Splitting the days by the market’s return lowers every subset’s correlation below the all-days figure.

Concentration in the Index

The cap-weighted index increasingly moves with these seven, and the equal-weighted index does not. The share of SPY’s daily variance explained by the equal-weight seven was 0.33 at the end of 2013, 0.84 at the end of 2022 and 0.71 on 10 November 2026; for RSP it is 0.22.

R² of a regression of each fund’s daily returns on the equal-weight basket’s over the trailing 252 trading days, at month ends. SPY weights the S&P 500 by market cap; RSP weights the same stocks equally. The data hold no historical index weights, so the seven’s weight in the index is not measured.

Who Carries the Risk

Equal weights do not mean equal risk. Over the full sample TSLA carries 21.0% of the equal-weight basket’s volatility and AAPL 10.6%; over the last year AAPL carries 5.2%.

Weights and covariance

Each stock’s share of the basket’s volatility is its weight times its marginal contribution, wi(Σw)i/σp; the shares add up to 100%. The diversification ratio is the weighted average of the stocks’ volatilities over the portfolio’s. Current market caps are Yahoo’s on 11 November 2026 and are used only with the last year’s covariance.

Joint Tail Moves

Joint crashes cluster more than correlation alone implies. On 35 days at least three of the seven fell more than 5%; a Gaussian copula with the observed rank correlations expects 27, and independent stocks fewer than one. On 10 days at least five fell more than 5%, 2.9 times the 3.5 the copula expects.

Event Study: Nvidia, November 2026

Nvidia’s November 2026 sell-off was mostly company-specific, but not on its worst day. On 5 November NVDA fell 9.3%; SPY’s 4.4% fall, at a beta of 1.89, accounts for −8.3 points. On 9 November, the first trading day after its board’s statement, NVDA fell 4.3% while SPY rose 2.4%, and its company-specific part was −8.4% (z −5.0). A semiconductor basket without Nvidia in place of SMH gives the same reading of both days, but changes the DeepSeek day, 27 January 2025, from mostly sector to mostly company-specific.

From its highest close, on 6 October 2026, to 10 November 2026, Nvidia fell 29.4% while SPY fell 3.7%. On 5 November SPY fell 4.4% and SMH, a semiconductor ETF, 11.2%. On Sunday 8 November Nvidia’s board said it was opening an independent investigation, overseen by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel (statement from Nvidia’s board). 9 November was the first trading day after the statement.

Each day’s return is split into four parts that add up to it: market (beta to SPY times SPY’s return), sector (the semiconductor factor’s move beyond what SPY implies), alpha, and a company-specific part. The coefficients come from the 250 trading days ending 21 days before each event. z is the company-specific part over the residual standard deviation of that window.

Data and Caveats

  • Prices are daily closes from Yahoo Finance via the yfinance library, adjusted for splits and dividends; factors come from the Ken French data library. Yahoo data is for personal use, so this page shows only the summary statistics published in the repository: no price or return series.
  • The seven were chosen with hindsight because they became the largest companies. Returns, Sharpe ratios and alphas measured on them overstate what a forward-looking investor could have earned. The risk and co-movement results are less affected, but also describe stocks that turned out to be winners.
  • SPY, QQQ and RSP are funds, so their returns are after fees. The equal-weight portfolios trade free of cost.
  • The value-weighted Ken French factors include these seven, which flatters the factor R² and the residual correlations. The factor files end on 30 September 2026.
  • One-year rolling windows jump when an extreme day enters and again when it leaves, 252 days later.
  • The event days were chosen after the fact. The z-scores are descriptive: Nvidia’s residuals have fat tails, so they should not be read as normal p-values.

The page reads one JSON file that scripts/export_site_data.py builds from the CSVs in results/; a test checks that every date in it is a month end or one of the event days. The code, tests and full write-up are on GitHub.
